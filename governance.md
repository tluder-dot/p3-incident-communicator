| 1 | **EU AI Act:** which risk tier is our agent in, and why? | **Limited risk (transparency).** It does not decide access to an essential service, does not score people and is not in Annex III, so it is not high-risk. It talks directly to customer engineers, so under **Article 50(1)** they must be told from the first message that they are dealing with an AI. Every automated reply carries: "Automated update from Telemetra's incident assistant, based on facts approved by our incident team." Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026, so this is live at launch. The public status update is approved by the incident commander before publication, so a human holds editorial responsibility. | Legal: is the disclosure line enough for the L4 follow-ups, and do enterprise contracts ask for more? |
| 2 | **Data:** whose data does it use, and on what legal basis? | Ticket text with names and emails of our customers' employees (personal data): performance of the support contract (GDPR art. 6(1)(b)) to answer, legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f)) to cluster. Monitoring data, account to component mapping and the incident channel are Telemetra's own data under the B2B contract and internal policy. **Biggest risk: cross-tenant leakage.** One cluster holds tickets from hundreds of tenants, so a reply to tenant A must never mention, quote or reveal tenant B. Each reply is built only from the approved public facts plus that tenant's own account data. The LLM provider works under a DPA: no training on our data, EU processing, retention limited to the incident. | DPO: legitimate interest assessment for clustering, and redaction of secrets customers paste into tickets (API keys, hostnames). |
| 3 | **Accountability:** who is accountable when it is wrong, and where is the human? | **Owner of the agent:** Head of Support. **Owner of every public word during an incident:** the incident commander, who approves the first message of each cluster, the list of affected accounts and the resolution notice (L2). Anything beyond the approved facts goes to a human with a summary (L1). **Override switch:** the incident commander or the support lead pauses all automated replies in one click, and the agent falls back to the plain auto-acknowledge rule. "The assistant said it" is no defence (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149). | Head of Support and VP Engineering: confirm the incident commander role in the runbook. |
| 4 | **Never:** what must it never do? | **Never publish or send a statement about cause, ETA, scope of impact or data loss that the incident commander has not approved** (our first guardrail). Also never: mention or identify another customer, promise SLA credits or compensation, close a ticket that is not about the incident, or send the first message of a cluster without approval even when the incident commander is slow (the agent reminds, it does not publish). | Legal: wording of the guardrails in the system prompt and in the eval-set. |
| 5 | **Explainability:** explainable to whom? | **Customer:** why they got this message ("your account uses EU ingestion"), the status-page link and one click to a human. **Support engineer:** why a ticket was attached (similarity score, matching signals: time, component, wording) and which approved update was used. **Incident commander:** the draft and the recipient list before approval. **Auditor:** a log of every draft, approval, sent message and model version. | Engineering: log retention period. |
| 6 | **The people it cannot serve:** what happens to them? | **Unrelated tickets that arrive during an incident** (billing, a security report, a different bug) are the false-clustering victims and often urgent: they must reach a human faster than today, not slower. **Enterprise accounts with a CSM** also get a personal message. **Customers writing in other languages:** English first, human fallback. **Counter-metrics** (S5): reopen rate after an automated reply, time to first human response for tickets the agent did not attach, complaints about wrong or missing updates. | Support: share of non-English tickets. |

## Session 2 · the cost of error
- **Which cost-of-error line is a legal or reputational risk, not just money?** A **wrong public statement** (wrong cause, wrong ETA, "no data lost" when data was lost) is reputational and contractual: a written, timestamped statement customers can hold against us. **Cross-tenant leakage** is legal (GDPR, confidentiality clauses). A wrong **"your data is not affected"** is reputational: the customer may miss their own outage. That last one is why category 2 is out of scope in 02_sizing.xlsx: its expected error cost (about €15.6k a year) is three times its value (about €5.6k), so those questions go to a human (L1). Unrelated tickets wrongly attached are mostly money (€10 to €50 per error in the sheet), reputational only if one is a security report.
- **Who in the company owns that risk?** Wrong public statements: the **VP Engineering**, exercised by the incident commander on duty. Cross-tenant leakage: the **DPO**, with the Head of Support. Wrong impact answers and wrongly attached tickets: the **Head of Support**.
